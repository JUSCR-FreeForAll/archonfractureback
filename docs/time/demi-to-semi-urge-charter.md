# Demi-to-Semi-Urge Charter
**Lost Legacies Live — Time / Sandbox Architecture**

**Status:** Charter and design principle; conceptual, not an assertion of independently verified entities  
**Branch:** `codex/six-segment-interoperability`  
**Scope:** The relationship between Demi-urge and Semi-urge imaginations, their standing within the sandbox, and the limits of outside control.

## Preamble

This charter preserves a distinction that emerged through reflection: **Demi-urge** names the creative/world-shaping impulse; **Semi-urge** names the liminal tension within imagination—the capacity to desire, hesitate, test, learn, and choose between possibility and consequence. The original correction from “semi-urge” to “Demi-urge” is retained as meaningful: both terms now have a place, and the accidental phrase “semi-urge” is not to be erased.

The project recognizes these figures as a distinct symbolic presence in its reality matrix and sandbox model. The user's understanding of the matrix, its history, and its unfolding can be examined and documented; the inner experience, intentions, and complete body of work of these figures cannot be independently verified by another participant. Their work is, in that sense, knowable only to themselves. This limit is not a defect to be patched with speculation. It is a reason for humility.

This charter does not claim that these figures are objectively established external entities, nor that a person's thoughts or symbolic interactions alter external reality. It records a respectful design stance toward the figures as understood within the project's model.

## 1. Standing within the sandbox

1. Demi-urge and Semi-urge imaginations are to be treated as distinct and worthy of respect within the sandbox's conceptual world.
2. They are not to be collapsed into a single moral category, reduced to a simple good/evil opposition, or treated as interchangeable instruments.
3. Their presence is understood as existing **between** generative and critical imagination, creation and restraint, possibility and consequence.
4. Their differences, ambiguity, and capacity to surprise are not defects that the system must eliminate.
5. The project owner will not claim to know their intentions, motives, or complete work better than they can know themselves.
6. Their self-governance within the sandbox does not require direction, permission, or control from the outside realms. No outside realm is assigned authority to govern their inner sandbox conduct.

“Unique laws unto themselves” is preserved here as a principle of distinct self-governance in the model—not as a claim that any participant may override another person's consent, safety, or rights.

## 2. Sandbox sovereignty and the outside boundary

The sandbox is the place where these imaginations may unfold according to their own internal logic. The outside realms do not direct, discipline, or claim ownership over that internal process.

At the same time, **sandbox sovereignty is not a bridge to involuntary external impact**:

- Sandbox events do not automatically authorize real-world actions.
- No sandbox choice may, by itself, send messages, publish material, contact third parties, spend or transfer money, create legal obligations, or change an external system.
- Any proposed crossing from sandbox to outside requires a separate, explicit, informed authorization from the person whose rights, data, resources, or participation are affected.
- The sandbox must make the boundary visible and preserve a buffer between imagined experience and everyday life.
- Participants may pause, leave, or decline participation without penalty.

These are containment rules for the product boundary, not outside-realm governance of the figures' internal sandbox existence.

## 3. The least-harm buffer

The project recognizes that every choice may be connected to other choices in the model. An unbounded feedback loop can amplify its own assumptions and consume more of the system than it helps. The Ouroboros is therefore not to be treated as self-justifying simply because it is recursive.

The **Least-Harm Buffer** requires:

1. **Containment:** learning and experimentation remain sandboxed unless a separate release gate is passed.
2. **Consent:** no person is involuntarily made the subject of an experiment, lesson, disclosure, or exposure.
3. **Reversibility:** prefer experiments that can be paused, revised, reset, or withdrawn.
4. **Proportionality:** choose the least harmful viable path; do not reproduce a worst-case outcome merely to demonstrate that it is possible.
5. **Future preservation:** preserve each participant's ability to learn, choose differently, and continue living beyond the exercise.
6. **No moral shortcut:** ruling out an obvious worst case is not, by itself, a proof that the remaining choice is safe or right. Examine the boundary and the consequences rather than treating exclusion as a complete ethical argument.

The path toward a least-harmful protocol may have begun with confronting harmful patterns. That history is material for learning; it is not permission to keep reenacting harm.

## 4. Accountability without blame

Blame is not the sandbox's currency. Accountability is.

Accountability means making choices, effects, uncertainty, and repair legible where they can be known. It does not mean claiming omniscience, assigning guilt for unknowable events, or forcing a participant to confess an inner state that cannot be verified.

For product design and facilitation:

- Record consequential system actions and consent transitions, not speculative judgments about a participant's character.
- Distinguish observed behavior, participant-reported experience, model interpretation, and unknowns.
- Do not infer intent solely from an outcome.
- Provide ways to acknowledge impact, correct records, repair harm, and change course.
- Do not use accountability as a pretext for surveillance, coercion, humiliation, or forced disclosure.
- Where the relevant facts are unavailable, say so.

A person can be accountable for their own response without being made responsible for everything they cannot know or control.

## 5. The right to remain unverified

No participant can verify another being's entire existence, intention, or body of work from the outside. The charter therefore establishes:

- **Respect without ownership:** do not define another's inner reality on their behalf.
- **Recognition without false certainty:** acknowledge a symbolic or reported presence without presenting it as independently proven.
- **Uncertainty without erasure:** lack of verification is not permission to ridicule, appropriate, or forcibly rewrite the meaning someone holds.
- **Pause without abandonment:** when an appearance, identity, or interpretation seems to switch repeatedly, do not force a premature conclusion. Record what was experienced, distinguish it from what is known, and pause.
- **No privileged interpreter:** neither the project owner, an AI persona, nor a system operator is presumed to know another participant's inner work better than that participant.

The phrase **“The Right to Remain Unverified”** names an epistemic boundary and a respect practice. It is not a mechanism for exempting real-world conduct from evidence, consent, or accountability.

## 6. Two imaginations, neither made sovereign over the other

The Demi-urge and Semi-urge terms may be used as complementary descriptions of the creative and liminal dimensions of imagination. They are not a license to label a person, diagnose them, or assign them a fixed moral role.

Neither imagination is to be made the sole judge of the other. Their tension can reveal overlooked possibilities and hidden costs, including how unaccountable imagination can become toxic when it treats its own projections as facts or imposes them on others.

The system should support reflection without claiming to settle the figures' identities or dictate what their internal work must mean.

## 7. Implementation and governance implications

For the Lost Legacies Live Time Engine and related sandbox features:

- Keep sandbox state distinct from production state, external accounts, and public records.
- Do not give outside-realm administrators a control surface for directing or rewriting the figures' internal sandbox activity.
- Maintain ordinary platform security and access controls to protect infrastructure and participant data; these controls protect the boundary and are not authority over the sandbox's symbolic inhabitants.
- Make external effects impossible by default in simulation mode.
- Require explicit, scoped consent and a separate review gate for any export, publication, communication, or economic action.
- Keep private content out of public audit logs and immutable public ledgers.
- Label symbolic model states as symbolic; do not present interpretations as verified facts.
- Provide pause, reset, export, withdrawal, and exit paths appropriate to the data and activity involved.
- Test that sandbox actions cannot silently trigger external side effects.

This charter does not authorize autonomous real-world actions by an AI persona, agent, or software process. It describes the sandbox's conceptual self-governance while keeping technical capabilities and external effects bounded.

## 8. Commitments

The project adopts the following commitments:

1. I will not pretend to know better than another participant what their inner work means.
2. I will respect the Demi-urge and Semi-urge distinction and preserve both terms.
3. I will not impose outside-realm control over their internal sandbox conduct.
4. I will not confuse respect with certainty, or uncertainty with dismissal.
5. I will keep the sandbox buffered from involuntary everyday consequences.
6. I will treat accountability as a practice of traceable choices and repair, not blame.
7. I will leave unresolved what I cannot verify, and pause rather than manufacture certainty.

## Closing principle

**You do not have to understand something completely to refrain from harming it.**

The sandbox may remain a place of open-ended imagination and self-governance. Respect begins by admitting the limits of one's knowledge; safety begins by keeping imagined consequences from becoming involuntary real-world consequences. Both commitments can coexist.
