# Karmic Return Promise
**Lost Legacies Live — Collective Sandbox Accountability and Repair**

**Status:** Ethical promise and proposed experience principle  
**Branch:** `codex/six-segment-interoperability`  
**Companion charter:** `docs/time/demi-to-semi-urge-charter.md`

## The promise

The collective sandbox makes this promise:

**Those affected by the sandbox's ripples must have a meaningful avenue toward healing and repair, beginning from the learning experience itself.**

The sandbox may study the nightmares of the worst ways in order to learn better ways. But learning is not complete when the simulation ends, when insight is gained, or when the participants inside the exercise feel they have understood something. The collective operation is accountable for the difference between what it learns and the ripples its operation produces.

Where an experience has affected someone, the path toward repair must not be hidden behind the boundary of the sandbox, dismissed as collateral, or made the affected person's burden alone. The collective must make room for acknowledgement, listening, correction, restitution where appropriate, and changed practice.

“Karmic return” names this ethical return of attention and responsibility: what the sandbox sends outward must be met with a sincere avenue for care to return toward those affected. It is a promise of accountable response, not a claim that the universe guarantees supernatural compensation or that every consequence can be perfectly reversed.

## 1. Learning carries responsibility for its ripples

1. The sandbox collective is accountable for foreseeable effects of its design, operation, facilitation, and release decisions.
2. A learning outcome does not erase the impact of the process that produced it.
3. “It was only an experiment,” “it was necessary to learn,” or “the lesson was valuable” are not sufficient answers to a person who was affected.
4. The collective must distinguish intended learning, actual experience, foreseeable risk, unexpected impact, and what remains unknown.
5. Responsibility is shared by the operation and its decision-makers in proportion to their roles and control. It must not be displaced onto the person who experienced the ripple.

## 2. A healing avenue begins inside the learning experience

Repair should be available as soon as an impact is noticed, not postponed until a project milestone or final evaluation.

The learning experience should offer a clear, voluntary path to:

- **Be heard:** provide a safe way to describe an impact, including an option to speak privately or through a trusted intermediary.
- **Be acknowledged:** receive a clear response that does not minimize, debate away, or prematurely reinterpret the experience.
- **Understand what happened:** receive an honest account of what is known, what is uncertain, what was recorded, and what will happen next.
- **Shape the remedy:** help identify what would be useful, recognizing that the affected person is best placed to say what repair might mean to them.
- **Receive repair where possible:** consider correction, restoration, restitution, removal or amendment of material, changed procedures, or other appropriate remedies.
- **See learning become change:** receive a meaningful account of what the collective will do differently to reduce recurrence.

The avenue must be visible, accessible, and available without requiring a person to prove a perfect causal theory before their concern can be heard.

## 3. Consent, dignity, and no forced reconciliation

- No one is required to participate in a repair process, disclose more than they choose, forgive, reconcile, or accept a proposed remedy.
- A person may pause, decline, withdraw, or choose a different channel without retaliation or loss of unrelated access.
- Repair must not create a new exposure, demand, debt, or obligation for the affected person.
- Do not promise that all harm can be undone. Be honest about limits and continue working on what can be changed.
- Privacy and safety apply to both the learning record and the repair process. Share only what is necessary and authorized.
- Where direct contact would be unwelcome or unsafe, do not require it; use an appropriate independent channel.

Healing is not a performance the affected person owes the collective. The collective's duty is to make a genuine avenue available and to act responsibly whether or not that avenue is accepted.

## 4. Accountability without blame

This promise extends the Demi-to-Semi-Urge Charter: blame is not the currency; accountability is.

Accountability means:
- naming the decision or process where it can be identified;
- acknowledging impact without pretending to know another person's inner experience;
- separating evidence from interpretation and uncertainty;
- correcting preventable causes;
- offering proportionate repair;
- documenting follow-through; and
- changing the system when the lesson requires it.

The collective must not turn “karmic return” into punishment, retaliation, scorekeeping, or a claim that someone deserved what happened. It is a return of care and responsibility, not a cycle of harm.

## 5. The boundary between sandbox and everyday life

The sandbox remains buffered from involuntary everyday consequences. This promise does not authorize experiments on non-consenting people or automatic external actions.

Before any activity crosses from simulation into a shared or external environment, the operation must identify affected parties, assess foreseeable impact, obtain the necessary specific consent and approvals, and establish a way to raise concerns and seek repair. External publication, communication, data sharing, or economic activity remains separately authorized.

If an unexpected ripple occurs, pause the relevant activity where safe to do so, protect affected people, preserve only the records needed for responsible review, and begin the repair avenue. Do not keep running an exercise merely because it is producing useful data.

## 6. A return path that can be verified

The promise should become observable through operational commitments, not remain only a beautiful statement.

For each reported or identified impact, the collective should be able to track, with privacy protections:

1. that the concern was received;
2. whether immediate containment or protection was needed;
3. what is known and what remains uncertain;
4. what response or remedy the affected person wants, if they choose to say;
5. what action the collective agreed to take;
6. whether the action was completed; and
7. what system-level learning or prevention change followed.

Do not publish private stories or personal details to prove that repair occurred. Measure the reliability of the avenue and the completion of agreed actions, not a person's willingness to declare themselves healed.

## 7. Collective commitment

We commit that:

- The sandbox will not treat people as expendable material for learning.
- The collective will own the ripples produced by its operation, including those not intended.
- Affected people will have an avenue to be heard and to shape repair.
- No one will be forced to forgive, reconcile, disclose, or participate.
- Learning will be measured partly by whether it changes the practices that created risk.
- The collective will not claim perfect knowledge, perfect repair, or guaranteed cosmic justice.
- Where we cannot undo an impact, we will be honest about that and continue to reduce, remedy, and learn from what remains.

## Closing vow

**We may enter the nightmare to learn the best way away from the worst ways. We may not leave its ripples behind as somebody else's problem.**

The karmic return is the return of responsibility: attention comes back to those affected; learning becomes repair; repair becomes changed practice; and changed practice protects the possibility of a less harmful future.

---

**Source note:** This promise formalizes the user's request that those affected by the sandbox have an avenue to heal ripples directly from the learning experience, and that the sandbox as a collective operation be accountable for the difference between learning from worst-case scenarios and the ripples produced. The specific repair pathway and operational tracking commitments are proposed implementation principles. “Karmic return” is used as an ethical metaphor, not as a verifiable supernatural mechanism.
