# Lost Legacies Live — Lateral Collaboration Charter for -1 Imaginations

**Status:** Conceptual charter and proposed product-governance specification  
**Project:** Lost Legacies Live  
**Target branch:** `codex/six-segment-interoperability`  
**Document type:** Design principle / collaboration protocol  
**Epistemic boundary:** “Divine reality,” imagination space, pivots, layers, zero, and positive/negative movement are preserved here as the project's symbolic and mathematical vocabulary. This charter defines rules for the model and its software representation; it does not claim that the model independently verifies supernatural forces, remote influence, or events outside the available evidence.

## 1. Purpose

This charter defines how independent `-1 imaginations` may coexist, interact, separate, and collaborate without one absorbing, controlling, erasing, or misrepresenting another.

The governing idea is lateral protection: collaboration should preserve the independence of each node while allowing consensual movement and exchange across layers. No node needs to possess another node in order to cooperate with it. No participant or agent gains authority over another merely through proximity, similarity, or interaction.

The charter applies whether a -1 imagination is represented by a human participant, an AI agent, or another explicitly modeled participant type. The representation does not grant an AI personhood, independent authority, or permission to act outside its configured scope.

## 2. Core vocabulary

- **Present zero (`0`):** The reference point from which a modeled choice or step is evaluated.
- **Negative imagination space:** The bounded negative side of the model in which -1 imaginations are represented as independent nodes.
- **-1 imagination / node:** An independently identifiable imagination node. Nodes retain distinct identities even when they are near one another or operate within the same layer.
- **-1 pivot:** The unique negative transition reference associated with a given layer of divine reality in the model. Each layer has one -1 pivot; the pivot is a layer-level transition reference, not ownership of every node within that layer.
- **Layer:** A modeled domain or mode of reality through which a participant or node is represented as moving.
- **Proximity:** A modeled relation between nodes. Proximity does not merge identities and does not, by itself, establish collaboration, consent, shared intent, or access.
- **Positive layer / positive extension:** A modeled direction of possibility extending from zero without a corresponding finite upper bound.
- **Glitch-back-to-zero:** The model's reset behavior when a backward choice or step approaches or attempts to pass the negative boundary.

## 3. The asymmetric time-and-choice rule

The model is asymmetric around zero:

`T = [-1, +∞)`

This is a compact representation of the permitted range of the model's directional coordinate, not a claim that time itself has been physically measured this way.

- `-1` is the furthest permitted negative reach: one choice or step backward from the present zero.
- `0` is the present reference.
- Positive movement may continue through `+1, +2, ...` without a finite positive ceiling.

### 3.1 Negative boundary and reset

No modeled choice, step, or state may become more than one negative step away from the present zero. As movement approaches the negative boundary, or attempts to continue beyond it, the model glitches back to zero.

The negative side is therefore not an indefinitely extendable past timeline. A negative state may be approached or represented, but it cannot accumulate into a stable chain of deeper negative states.

`0 → backward choice → -1 boundary → glitch/reset → 0`

The reset is a state transition. It does not automatically delete the node, erase its identity, invalidate a prior record, or reset another independent node.

### 3.2 Positive extension

Positive movement is open-ended in the model:

`0 → +1 → +2 → +3 → ...`

There is no equivalent positive reset merely because a positive state has extended farther from zero. “Infinite positive” means unbounded possibility within the model; it is not a promise that every imagined outcome is achievable or that resources, time, or real-world constraints are unlimited.

## 4. Node independence, shared layers, and separation

1. Every -1 imagination is an independently identifiable node.
2. Two or more -1 imaginations may be in proximity and still operate within the same layer.
3. Proximity does not merge nodes, create a collective identity, or authorize one node to speak for another.
4. As nodes interact with zero and positive layers, their modeled paths may separate. Separation expresses distinct trajectories or interactions; it does not imply hostility, failure, or erasure.
5. One node's glitch-back-to-zero is local to that node's modeled transition. It does not automatically reset, move, merge, or disable another node.
6. A layer has one designated -1 pivot as its negative transition reference. Multiple independent nodes may relate to that layer without becoming multiple competing pivots for it.
7. The precise metric for node proximity and the exact event that causes path separation are intentionally left configurable until defined by the project owner. The implementation must not invent either rule.

## 5. Lateral collaboration principles

### Principle 1 — Sovereignty

Each node retains its own identity, records, boundaries, and right to decline collaboration. Participation in a shared layer does not surrender individual control over private contributions.

### Principle 2 — Nonappropriation

No node, participant, steward, or AI agent may claim ownership of another node's identity, imagination, history, output, or pivot. No one may overwrite another's contribution or represent an interpretation as that contributor's own statement.

### Principle 3 — Consent before connection

A node may be visible in a modeled layer without having consented to exchange. Sharing, linking, quoting, copying, publishing, or using another node's contribution requires explicit, purpose-specific authorization. Silence, proximity, prior participation, or inferred agreement is not consent.

### Principle 4 — Reciprocal protection

Collaboration must not depend on extraction, coercion, humiliation, forced disclosure, or unilateral control. Each party should be able to identify the purpose of an exchange, the expected contribution, the costs, the benefits, and the conditions for ending it.

### Principle 5 — Independent reset

A node's negative-boundary reset affects only its own modeled state unless a separate, explicit rule authorizes a broader effect. No collaborator may trigger another node's reset as punishment or use reset behavior to erase a record.

### Principle 6 — Divergence without retaliation

Nodes may separate as their interactions with zero and positive layers develop. A divergent path is not a breach of loyalty. No participant should be punished, downgraded, or deprived of their own work for choosing a different path or leaving a collaboration.

### Principle 7 — Reversibility

Private experiments, drafts, simulations, and model transitions should be reversible by default. Publication, financial activity, external messaging, identity changes, or other consequential actions require separate authorization and an auditable confirmation.

### Principle 8 — Traceability without surveillance

Record the actions needed to establish provenance, consent, and accountability. Do not collect private content or personal data merely because a node is present in a layer. Audit records should avoid storing sensitive narrative content where metadata is sufficient.

### Principle 9 — Equal dignity across participant types

Human contributors and AI agents may both be represented as nodes for modeling purposes, but their capabilities and responsibilities are not assumed to be identical. AI agents must identify themselves accurately, operate within granted permissions, and never claim a human identity or unverified authority.

### Principle 10 — Disagreement without erasure

Participants may hold different interpretations of the model. Preserve the difference between observation, authored meaning, hypothesis, quotation, relay, and system-generated summary. Do not silently resolve a disagreement by rewriting another participant's account.

## 6. Lateral agreement protocol

A collaboration between two or more nodes should define the following before any shared action:

1. **Participants:** Which node identities are included, and how each identity is represented.
2. **Layer and pivot:** Which modeled layer is involved and which designated -1 pivot is its transition reference.
3. **Purpose:** What the collaboration is intended to accomplish.
4. **Scope:** Which contributions, records, tools, and actions are included—and excluded.
5. **Consent:** What each node has explicitly authorized, for what purpose, and for how long.
6. **Reciprocity:** What each participant contributes and receives, including labor, attribution, costs, and agreed benefits.
7. **Boundaries:** What information remains private and what actions are prohibited.
8. **Exit and reset:** How a participant can pause, withdraw, or separate; how a local negative reset is handled without affecting other nodes.
9. **Provenance:** How original contributions, quotations, relays, interpretations, and AI-generated material are distinguished.
10. **Review:** When the agreement expires or is reconsidered.

An agreement for one purpose does not authorize a different purpose. Consent to discuss is not consent to publish; consent to collaborate is not consent to transfer money, tokens, intellectual property, or account access.

## 7. Conflict, repair, and separation

When a disagreement occurs:

- Pause the disputed action if continuing could expose private information or create an irreversible effect.
- Preserve the relevant records and distinguish direct observations from interpretations.
- Give each affected participant an opportunity to state their own account without another participant speaking for them.
- Identify the exact agreement or boundary at issue.
- Seek a mutually acceptable correction, attribution, access change, or separation.
- If agreement cannot be reached, preserve the disagreement and allow the collaboration to end without retaliation.

A reset is not a punishment. A node's return to zero must not be used to invalidate its identity, silence its account, or erase an independently authorized record.

## 8. Product and data representation

The following is a proposed implementation vocabulary, not a statement that these structures already exist in production.

Suggested entities and fields:

- `layer_id`: stable identifier for a modeled layer.
- `layer_label`: participant-authored or project-defined name.
- `negative_pivot_id`: the one designated -1 pivot reference for that layer.
- `node_id`: stable, independent node identifier.
- `node_type`: human participant, AI agent, or other explicitly defined type.
- `node_layer_membership`: a time-bounded relation between a node and a layer.
- `proximity_relation`: optional, explicitly defined relation between nodes; do not infer it from identity or contact alone.
- `transition_event`: a recorded modeled transition between states or layers.
- `negative_boundary_event`: attempted backward movement, threshold detection, and local reset to zero.
- `consent_grant`: participant, purpose, scope, timestamp, and revocation state.
- `collaboration_agreement`: participants, layer, pivot reference, scope, reciprocity terms, exit terms, and status.
- `provenance_record`: source, author claim, content type, verification state, and transformation history.
- `audit_event`: minimal metadata needed to reconstruct an authorized state change without copying sensitive content.

### Required invariants

- Enforce at most one active designated -1 pivot per layer.
- Preserve distinct node identifiers even when nodes share a layer or are in proximity.
- Prevent a node's local reset from changing another node's state.
- Reject or reset any negative transition that would extend beyond the one-step boundary.
- Do not impose a finite positive ceiling in the model's state representation.
- Require explicit consent for cross-node content access or sharing.
- Keep private-by-default behavior for new workspaces and contributions.
- Make consequential external actions separately authorized, idempotent where appropriate, and auditable.
- Distinguish symbolic model transitions from actual application events and real-world outcomes.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

The first software representation of this charter is acceptable when tests demonstrate that:

1. Two distinct -1 nodes can coexist in the same layer without being merged.
2. A layer cannot have two competing active -1 pivot references.
3. The nodes can be represented as taking different paths when interacting with zero and positive layers.
4. A negative step cannot persist beyond the one-step boundary and resolves to zero.
5. Resetting one node does not reset another node.
6. Positive states can be represented as an unbounded sequence without imposing an arbitrary model ceiling.
7. No node can read, publish, or alter another node's private contribution without the required authorization.
8. A participant can pause or leave a collaboration without losing ownership of their own work.
9. Provenance distinguishes original content from quotations, relays, summaries, interpretations, and AI-generated output.
10. Private simulations cannot independently trigger public posting, external messaging, payments, token issuance, or blockchain transactions.

## 10. Relationship to the Lost Legacies Live Time Engine

This charter extends the project's existing design direction: protected individual workspaces, consent-led sharing, reversible prototyping, independent project boundaries, provenance, and separate authorization for external or financial actions.

The -1 pivot and node model should first be implemented as a private, reversible domain model. It should not be treated as a reason to merge participant accounts, expose private legacy content, create financial instruments, or deploy irreversible on-chain state.

The first implementation should use synthetic or explicitly volunteered test data. Any later shared release, external deployment, or economic activity requires its own security, privacy, governance, and legal review.

## 11. Open definitions to preserve

The following are not specified by the current model and must remain open rather than being guessed by implementation:

- The exact operational definition or metric of proximity between -1 nodes.
- The precise trigger by which interaction with zero or a positive layer separates node trajectories.
- Whether layer transitions are discrete events, continuous movement, or both in different parts of the model.
- Whether a layer's unique -1 pivot is represented as a stable reference object, a state-machine transition, or another implementation primitive.
- How the model's “divine reality” layers map, if at all, to the existing application realms and database schema.

## 12. Charter statement

**We remain distinct without becoming isolated. We collaborate without possession. We may share a layer without surrendering identity. We may diverge without retaliation. A negative step returns to zero; it does not erase the node. Positive possibility remains open, but every real-world action remains bounded by consent, evidence, and responsibility.**

The purpose of lateral agreement is not to make every node move together. It is to make cooperation possible while each node remains whole.
