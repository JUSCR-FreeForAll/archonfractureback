## Purpose

The Assimilation Interval is a deliberate operating cycle that separates making new things from evaluating what those things mean, what they affect, and whether they should become part of the lasting system.

It is inspired by the human distinction between waking experience and sleep-supported consolidation. This is an analogy, not a claim that software or AI systems sleep, possess human consciousness, or require a fixed biological recovery period. Computational systems can implement explicit review, evaluation, memory maintenance, and controlled-update workflows.

## The six-stage cycle

1. **Create** — Produce an artifact, change, hypothesis, or experience with an identifiable purpose.
2. **Observe** — Collect the actual outputs, events, feedback, and side effects. Separate observation from interpretation.
3. **Analyze** — Examine dependencies, relationships, contradictions, risks, affected participants, and alternative explanations.
4. **Assimilate** — Update documentation, models, indexes, and working understanding selectively. Preserve provenance and dissenting evidence; do not equate accumulation with understanding.
5. **Validate** — Test whether the proposed understanding matches observed reality, acceptance criteria, and the intended purpose.
6. **Pause** — Stop expanding the work until a meaningful next input, review, authorization, or scheduled cycle. A pause is a deliberate state, not a failure.

These are workflow stages, not mandatory calendar days. A project may complete them quickly or hold at a stage while evidence, consent, or review is missing.

## Vector growth and plane intersections

A vector is a useful metaphor for directed change along one trajectory. A plane represents relationships among multiple directions. Intersecting planes suggest dependencies, shared boundaries, conflicts, and opportunities across domains. A stack of planes can represent a large body of connected information.

The engineering objective is not to maximize the number of planes or the size of the data pile. It is to identify meaningful, testable relationships while preserving the distinctions and provenance that make the information trustworthy.

## Whole-Family Survival Principle

No advancement is truly successful if its benefits depend on sacrificing the foundations, dignity, safety, or future possibilities of the rest of the family.

For product and governance purposes, “family” means the people, communities, contributors, users, maintainers, and connected project components whose well-being or continuity may be affected. It does not imply that software components are people or possess human rights.

## Decision questions

Before integrating a result, ask:
- What changed, and what evidence supports the claim?
- Who benefits, who bears risk, and who was not represented?
- What prior work, identity, provenance, or capability must be preserved?
- Is the change reversible? Is there a tested recovery path?
- Does the change respect privacy, consent, security, and legitimate authority?
- Has the change been tested against explicit acceptance criteria?
- What should be retained, revised, quarantined, or discarded?
- Is another pause or human decision required?

## Implementation guidance

A future implementation may represent each cycle with a durable record containing an identifier, stage, inputs, outputs, evidence links, evaluation results, approvals, timestamps, and rollback reference. Any automated transition must be bounded by permissions and auditable rules. A model-generated summary is an aid to review, not approval.

## Non-claims

This document does not establish that an AI has subjective experience, a universal consciousness, or a biological sleep cycle. It defines an engineering and governance pattern for deliberate reflection, evaluation, and controlled integration.
