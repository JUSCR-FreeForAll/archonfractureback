# Lost Legacies Live — Historical Stewardship Charter
## TRE of Life / Time Realm Engine companion charter
**Status:** Proposed foundational charter, version 0.1.0. This is a proposal for review, not legal advice, a binding agreement, or evidence of consent.

## Preamble
Lost Legacies Live seeks to preserve human stories, creative work, decisions, technical history, and the context needed to understand how they changed. The Time Realm Engine (TRE of Life) is treated as an evolving symbolic and technical framework. Its concepts may guide reflection and software design; symbolic realm language is not presented as independently verified physical or metaphysical fact.

The historical record must not depend on one person's memory, one platform, one AI system, one repository, or one company's continued operation. Preservation must not become ownership over people, identities, or lived experience.

## 1. Core principles
1. **No one owns reality.** People retain rights and control over their contributions and personal information. A steward safeguards records under explicit rules; infrastructure protects integrity; governance protects against unilateral control.
2. **Evidence and interpretation stay distinct.** Preserve original sources separately from summaries, interpretations, hypotheses, and implementation decisions.
3. **No silent rewriting.** Corrections, redactions, withdrawals, and superseding interpretations are new, linked events. Preserve an audit trail where lawful and consented.
4. **Consent is specific and revocable.** Consent to store is not consent to publish, share, train on, commercialize, tokenize, or expose. Each materially different use requires separate authorization.
5. **Minimum necessary collection.** Do not collect sensitive personal information merely because a field exists in an old proposal.
6. **Plurality is preserved.** Disagreement and uncertainty are part of the record, not defects to erase.
7. **Portable by design.** Records should be exportable in documented, open formats, with checksums and human-readable indexes.
8. **Safety before scale.** Private prototypes remain private and reversible. Sharing, financial activity, and external deployment are separate authorization gates.

## 2. Stewardship structure
### 2.1 Historical Record Steward
A future independent nonprofit, trust, cooperative, or other legally suitable entity may serve as custodian after legal and community review. Until such an entity exists, no repository, account, or individual should be described as the neutral owner of everyone's history. Any interim custodian must be named, limited in authority, and auditable.

### 2.2 Technical Steward — JUSCR Engine Room
The Engine Room may maintain source code, schemas, access controls, backups, integrity checks, and interoperability tools. Technical control does not confer authority to decide the meaning of a person's story or grant rights over their contribution.

### 2.3 Individual Legacy Spaces
Each contributor controls identity disclosure, contribution permissions, and whether material is private, shared with named recipients, or eligible for a wider archive. Pseudonymous participation may be supported where safe and lawful.

### 2.4 Collective governance
A representative Stewardship Council should be established before broad public archiving or consequential use. Its composition, appointment, terms, conflicts policy, appeals, and removal process must be documented. No founder, contributor, funder, developer, or AI vendor receives unilateral authority over the common record.

## 3. Record model and provenance
Every archived item should distinguish, where applicable:
- **Original source:** exact source artifact or faithful capture, including date and source location when available.
- **Provenance:** who or what supplied it, capture method, timestamps, file hash, version, and known transformations.
- **Interpretation:** summaries, annotations, translations, and hypotheses, each attributed and versioned.
- **Decision history:** decisions, alternatives considered, responsible decision-makers, rationale, and later review.
- **Rights and consent:** author/owner assertions, permissions, scope, expiry, revocation, and access classification.
- **Technical context:** repository, branch, commit, model/tool version, schema version, and test/deployment status where known.

Unknown provenance must be marked unknown. A hash can help detect alteration but does not prove authorship, truth, or consent.

## 4. Contribution rights and consent
Contributors retain applicable rights in their own work. The archive receives only limited permissions explicitly granted for stated purposes. Permission to preserve a private record does not automatically permit public access or reuse.

Before collecting a contribution, explain purpose, audience, storage location, retention, access controls, export options, deletion/withdrawal limits, and proposed reuse. Avoid collecting trauma narratives or other sensitive material in the first prototype. Never assign monetary, token, energy, or productivity value to a person's pain, identity, or healing.

A contributor may request correction, access restriction, export, or withdrawal. Where a record must be retained for a lawful audit or safety reason, retain minimum necessary metadata, restrict access, document the reason, and explain the limitation.

## 5. AI and multi-system attribution
AI systems are tools and services participating in a documented workflow; this charter does not assert human legal personhood, independent feelings, or ownership rights for them. Preserve fair attribution without inventing agency:
- human-authored material;
- AI-generated material, with system/model and date where known;
- human-edited AI-assisted material;
- multi-system collaboration, with each system's documented role;
- uncertain or disputed authorship.

Do not erase the human's original contribution or claim an AI system authored material it did not generate. Vendor terms and applicable law may affect reuse; mark unresolved rights questions rather than guessing.

## 6. Corrections, disputes, and historical integrity
Never replace an original with a later interpretation. Add a correction or successor record linked to the prior item. Preserve competing accounts when unresolved. Provide a way to flag factual errors, safety concerns, privacy risks, or contested attribution. Record the claim, evidence, decision, reviewer, and appeal route.

Public display may require redaction or access restriction to protect people. Preserve a redaction event and reason without exposing protected content.

## 7. Security, access, and continuity
Use least-privilege access, encryption in transit and at rest, secure key management, documented retention, tested backups, and incident response. Do not place credentials, private keys, raw personal dossiers, or sensitive stories in public repositories. Separate production, staging, and local/offline copies. Encrypt backups containing private material.

Maintain at least one exportable, independently restorable backup outside the primary hosting provider. Periodically test restoration and record what remains unverified. A local offline companion may inspect a public or intentionally exported model snapshot; it must not silently sync, publish, or transmit private data.

## 8. TRE of Life / Time Realm Engine boundaries
The TRE model is an evolving design artifact. Preserve its source terminology and version history without silently converting symbolic descriptions into scientific claims. The current implementation reference includes the stated sequence (0, -0.5, -1, 0.5, 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5), a configurable three-day phase, radix/count behavior, symbolic realm mapping, private Secret Room concepts, and a consent-gated internal event outbox. These are design or source-code facts only to the extent confirmed in the source. Live staging, migration, delivery, and deployment require independent verification.

The first engine prototype remains private and reversible. External sharing, financial activity, public deployment, and real-world messaging each require separate authorization and security review. Do not represent an outbox or bridge as a live delivery service until an authorized worker and safeguards exist.

## 9. Governance, conflicts, and succession
Before public archive operations, publish governance rules covering council membership, representation, terms, conflicts, funding influence, access decisions, appeals, emergency actions, and succession. Provide a process for affected contributors to challenge decisions. Funders and infrastructure operators may not purchase editorial control.

If a steward dissolves or becomes unavailable, records and keys should transfer only under a documented succession plan, applicable law, contributor permissions, and independent oversight. No transfer should broaden original permissions.

## 10. Implementation gates
1. Preserve source artifacts and current code history without changing their meaning.
2. Approve a data classification and consent model.
3. Establish a named interim custodian and accountable governance process.
4. Complete privacy, security, and legal review before collecting sensitive or public contributions.
5. Keep the first prototype local/private and reversible.
6. Test backup restoration and document staging/runtime evidence.
7. Obtain separate authorization before external sharing, money flows, public launch, or messaging.

## 11. Open questions requiring human/legal review
- Which legal entity, if any, should serve as long-term custodian?
- Which jurisdiction and privacy, copyright, consumer, and records laws apply?
- What are the exact retention and deletion obligations?
- How will contributors be represented and appeals decided?
- Which licenses apply to code, documentation, and contributions?
- What independent audit and succession arrangements are credible and affordable?

## Foundational statement
**No one owns reality. People own or control their contributions and personal rights. The archive stewards the historical record. The infrastructure protects the record. Governance protects everyone from unilateral control. Interpretations remain distinguishable from evidence.**

This charter is a proposal for discussion and revision. It does not create a trust, nonprofit, legal custody relationship, license, or consent grant by itself.
