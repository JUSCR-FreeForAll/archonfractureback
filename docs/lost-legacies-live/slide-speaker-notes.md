# Lost Legacies Live — Slide Speaker Notes and Visual Direction

Use with investor-deck.md. Keep slides concise; speaker notes carry nuance. Visuals should feel like a coherent enterprise technology story, not fantasy branding alone.

## Design system

- Format: 16:9 widescreen.
- Palette: midnight navy, warm ember gold, restrained teal, off-white.
- Typography: modern sans-serif; one display face for slide titles, one highly legible body face.
- Motif: a fine-line phoenix / rising signal made from connected nodes; use sparingly as a portfolio mark.
- Diagram language: six product nodes around a shared JUSCR Spine; distinguish shared platform services from product-owned data.
- Accessibility: high contrast, large labels, no information conveyed by color alone.
- Avoid: fake product screenshots, invented customer logos, unverified market charts, decorative blockchain imagery that implies production integrations.

## Slide-by-slide notes

### 1. Vision
**Visual:** Minimal cover; luminous phoenix-like network motif on dark navy, with generous negative space for the title.
**Speaker note:** “Lost Legacies Live is a portfolio strategy: independent experiences, one reusable and consent-led technology spine. The value is not a single super-app; it is the ability to build shared capabilities once and apply them across distinct products.”

### 2. Portfolio
**Visual:** Six distinct cards or nodes orbiting a central JUSCR hub. Give each product a unique accent icon, not a separate visual brand system.
**Speaker note:** “These are six strategic segments with different users and workflows. The map is the intended portfolio structure; integration and commercial maturity are tracked product by product, not assumed.”

### 3. Problem
**Visual:** Three-column contrast: fragmented systems, cloud-only experience, uncontrolled AI context. Use simple line icons and short labels.
**Speaker note:** “The recurring cost is rebuilding identity, permissions, synchronization, and provenance. Connectivity and data-control expectations make that fragmentation more visible. We are solving the shared infrastructure problem without erasing product boundaries.”

### 4. Product system
**Visual:** Layered architecture: six product experiences at the top, shared API/identity/consent/semantic/audit services in the middle, bounded data stores at the bottom.
**Speaker note:** “The shared spine enforces common policy and interoperability. It does not mean every product reads every other product’s data. Access remains scoped and consented.”

### 5. Technology differentiation
**Visual:** Two-column “Current foundation” versus “Target capabilities”; label current versus proposed explicitly.
**Speaker note:** “We already have a Fastify and PostgreSQL-oriented foundation with schema, consent, audit, and verification work. Local SQLite, CRDT synchronization, and MCP are the next architecture targets, not completed claims. We will add them incrementally behind tests.”

### 6. Why now
**Visual:** A simple convergence diagram with offline-first, semantic interoperability, and agentic workflows converging on trusted user-controlled experiences.
**Speaker note:** “The timing thesis is that responsive local experiences and bounded AI access are becoming more important. We will substantiate named market examples with primary-source research before external use.”

### 7. Business model
**Visual:** Three stacked revenue layers: applications, platform services, strategic IP. Use arrows to show reuse, not guaranteed revenue.
**Speaker note:** “Application revenue validates individual products. Platform revenue monetizes shared capabilities if customers value them. IP licensing is a later option, subject to ownership and licensing diligence.”

### 8. Market opportunity
**Visual:** Five adjacent customer segments as labeled circles; avoid unsupported TAM figures.
**Speaker note:** “These are potential markets, not a claim that we will pursue all of them at once. The next commercial decision is selecting one beachhead with an urgent workflow and identifiable buyer.”

### 9. Roadmap
**Visual:** Four-stage 24-month timeline with gates and measurable exit criteria.
**Speaker note:** “The roadmap is gated: prove offline sync, then trust controls and pilots, then paid conversion, then portfolio expansion. Dates and pilot counts are targets contingent on hiring and partner access.”

### 10. Readiness
**Visual:** Evidence matrix with statuses such as defined, in progress, proposed, and evidence needed. Do not use a blanket green “production ready” indicator.
**Speaker note:** “We are deliberately separating what exists from what is proposed. Revenue, retention, paid pilots, independent security review, and compliance evidence are diligence items, not implied achievements.”

### 11. Capital plan
**Visual:** Simple allocation bar or donut using the six budget categories and exact dollar amounts from the deck.
**Speaker note:** “The $2 million case is an illustrative seed scenario. The allocation is a starting model; the final budget will tie each tranche to hires, milestones, vendor costs, and runway.”

### 12. The ask
**Visual:** Return to the phoenix/network motif, now showing the shared spine connecting six product nodes. End with one clear investment ask.
**Speaker note:** “We are asking investors to fund proof before scale: one repeatable integration, one demonstrated offline-to-online workflow, measurable trust controls, and customer evidence. Expansion follows evidence.”

## Export checklist

- Preserve the “current / target / proposed” distinctions on slides 5 and 10.
- Keep the six product names consistent with the portfolio map.
- Verify every product URL, screenshot, usage metric, and logo before insertion.
- Add footnotes for every external market or competitor claim.
- Reconcile the capital allocation percentages and amounts.
- Include a dated appendix with benchmark methodology, customer evidence, IP register, and security review status.
- Remove any claim that cannot be traced to a source or owner.
