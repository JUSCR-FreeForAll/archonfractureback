# Lost Legacies Live — Investor Presentation

**Working investor narrative | 4 November 2026**

> Positioning discipline: this deck distinguishes the portfolio thesis, the current JUSCR Spine foundation, and the proposed sovereign-infrastructure roadmap. Product revenue, user traction, cost savings, compliance status, and market-size claims require evidence before external fundraising use.

## 1. Vision — One ecosystem, independent experiences

Lost Legacies Live is a portfolio of human-centered digital experiences built on shared, reusable infrastructure. Each product keeps its identity, audience, and commercial path while interoperating through common identity, consent, provenance, semantic contracts, and navigation.

**Thesis:** build trusted infrastructure once; let multiple products use it without collapsing their identities or ownership boundaries.

## 2. Portfolio — Six connected segments

1. **JUSCR Unified Living Ecosystem** — central discovery, identity entry point, and mobile-first portfolio hub.
2. **Phoenix Flies / PhoenixflyLive** — experiential storytelling and resilient-recovery realm.
3. **TimeAuction** — time-based marketplace and exchange experience.
4. **Karen Kuality Kontrol / KarenQuest** — quality, experience, and quest-oriented product.
5. **JUSCR-FreForAll** — community, collaboration, and shared participation.
6. **JustUsScool / 2foreachofu** — collaborative learning and co-creation.

The six-segment portfolio is a strategic map, not a claim that all six products are integrated, independently monetized, or production-ready today. Each segment advances through explicit product, privacy, and commercial gates.

## 3. Problem — Fragmented experiences and duplicated infrastructure

- Independent products repeatedly rebuild identity, permissions, data exchange, and audit behavior.
- Cloud-dependent experiences can become unavailable when connectivity is poor.
- AI features need useful context without receiving unrestricted access to private records.
- Data silos make provenance, portability, and cross-product discovery difficult.
- Centralized infrastructure can create avoidable cost and operational concentration.

The opportunity is to make shared infrastructure reusable while preserving participant control and product boundaries.

## 4. Product system — Shared spine, distinct realms

JUSCR is the portfolio entry point; the six segments remain distinct product experiences. A shared platform can provide identity federation, consent and policy enforcement, provenance, event contracts, audit records, and navigation. Product-specific workflows and data remain within their own bounded domains.

**Integration rule:** products consume reviewed APIs and schemas; they do not silently share unrestricted databases or duplicate the central policy engine.

## 5. Differentiation — A staged sovereign-infrastructure roadmap

- **Current foundation:** Fastify TypeScript service, PostgreSQL migration path, Zod validation, consent-ledger and audit-oriented contracts, JSON-LD / CloudEvents interoperability work, and a zero-knowledge verification boundary.
- **Target capabilities:** local-first client storage, offline outbox, CRDT-based conflict reconciliation, semantic context, scoped MCP tools, and verifiable audit chains.
- **Trust model:** explicit consent, least privilege, revocation, provenance, and auditable state transitions.

The target stack is a roadmap. Local SQLite, CRDT relays, MCP, Supabase, and Solana must be evaluated and integrated only where they solve a demonstrated requirement.

## 6. Why now — Convergence of local-first and agentic software

Users and organizations increasingly expect responsive offline-capable applications, portable data, interoperable systems, and constrained AI access. Lost Legacies Live can address these needs through an incremental architecture rather than a wholesale platform replacement.

Before publication, substantiate named market examples, acquisition claims, financing events, and competitor comparisons with dated primary sources.

## 7. Business model — Three layers

**Application revenue:** subscriptions, memberships, marketplace transaction fees, premium experiences, and product-specific services.

**Platform revenue:** shared synchronization, identity, administration, semantic APIs, and developer services, potentially priced by usage or tenant.

**Strategic IP:** licensing of reviewed schemas, workflow components, agent tools, and verification services where ownership and third-party licenses permit.

No revenue forecast is implied until pricing, conversion, retention, transaction volume, and customer willingness-to-pay are validated.

## 8. Market opportunity — Multiple adjacent entry points

Potential customer and user groups include creator and experiential communities, collaborative learning, marketplace participants, organizations with privacy-sensitive workflows, and developers building offline-capable or agent-connected applications.

The initial go-to-market should select one beachhead segment, identify a buyer and urgent workflow, and measure willingness to pay. Avoid adding TAM figures without a cited methodology and defensible serviceable-market assumptions.

## 9. Roadmap — 24 months, gated by evidence

**Phase 1 — Foundation (0–6 months):** consolidate repository contracts; ship one web/mobile entry point; prove local persistence and multi-device synchronization in a controlled pilot; establish consent and audit tests.

**Phase 2 — Trust and pilots (6–12 months):** harden authorization, revocation, replay protection, observability, and backup/recovery; introduce scoped MCP tools; run 3–5 design-partner pilots if qualified partners are secured.

**Phase 3 — Commercial validation (12–18 months):** instrument usage and cost; test pricing; convert pilots to paid recurring or transaction revenue; improve onboarding and support.

**Phase 4 — Expansion (18–24 months):** open documented integration surfaces, onboard additional portfolio products, and pursue institutional partnerships based on validated demand.

Milestones are targets, not commitments or current traction.

## 10. Readiness — Evidence-first status

- Portfolio strategy and six-segment map: defined as a strategic plan.
- JUSCR Spine API: existing Fastify/PostgreSQL-oriented codebase; inspect and test each integration branch before representing functionality as complete.
- Cross-repository integration: staged and contract-led; independent consumer app is not assumed connected.
- Local-first SQLite/CRDT implementation: proposed, not yet demonstrated by this document.
- MCP agent perimeter: proposed, not yet demonstrated by this document.
- Revenue, retention, paid pilots, and customer references: evidence required.
- SOC 2 / HIPAA: no certification or compliance claim. Architecture readiness is not certification.

## 12. The ask — Fund the proof, then scale the portfolio

Lost Legacies Live aims to unite distinct human-centered products through a shared, consent-led, interoperable technology spine. The immediate investment case is to prove one repeatable product-to-platform integration, validate user demand, and establish measurable security and operating economics.

**The request:** fund the next stage of engineering and commercial validation, with capital released against demonstrable product, security, and customer milestones.

## Investor diligence checklist

- Evidence of ownership, contributor assignments, and third-party license obligations for all material IP.
- Product inventory with live URLs, owners, user counts, engagement, and operating status.
- Customer discovery notes, pilot letters, contracts, revenue, retention, and pipeline evidence.
- Architecture and threat model reviewed by an independent security practitioner.
- Reproducible benchmark methodology for latency, sync reliability, and infrastructure cost.
- Financial model with hiring assumptions, runway, use of funds, and downside scenario.
- Legal review of privacy, consumer protection, marketplace, education, and regulated-sector obligations.
