# Whole-Family Survival Principle
**Status:** Foundational project principle  
**Applies to:** Lost Legacies Live and its connected projects, subject to each project's lawful authority and governance.

## Governing principle

No advancement is truly successful if its benefits depend on sacrificing the foundations, dignity, safety, or future possibilities of the rest of the family.

“Whole family” refers to the people and communities affected by the work: contributors, users, participants, maintainers, partners, and those who may bear downstream consequences. The phrase is an ethical and organizational commitment, not a claim that software systems are human family members.

## Five disciplines

1. **Preserve the lineage.** Protect original work, records, attribution, identity, context, and provenance. Never silently overwrite or erase prior contributions. Keep backups and migration histories.
2. **Find the better path.** Compare new possibilities against prior approaches using evidence, explicit criteria, and known trade-offs. Newer, faster, larger, or more complex does not automatically mean better.
3. **Protect vulnerable intersections.** Identify who bears risks where people, systems, data, resources, and responsibilities meet. Give particular attention to people with less power or less ability to opt out.
4. **Distribute resilience.** Avoid unnecessary single points of failure. Maintain independent recovery paths, backups, documented ownership, access continuity, and tested restoration procedures.
5. **Make growth accountable.** Test consequences before irreversible changes. Make trade-offs visible, preserve meaningful dissent, and require the appropriate human authorization for consequential decisions.

## Governing question

After everything we have created, learned, changed, and discovered, have we found a path that leaves the whole family more capable of surviving the next day?

## Operational interpretation

- Safety and dignity are constraints, not optional optimization targets.
- Consent must be specific, informed, revocable where applicable, and recorded appropriately.
- No one may declare that all affected people are satisfied on their behalf.
- Affected people need accessible ways to question, correct, decline, or leave.
- Project continuity must not depend on one person, one vendor, one model, one credential, or one deployment.
- High-impact decisions require human review and an accountable owner.
- If evidence is insufficient, preserve the uncertainty and pause rather than invent certainty.

## Conflict handling

This principle does not mean every preference can be satisfied simultaneously. When interests conflict, document the affected parties, evidence, legal and safety constraints, alternatives, decision owner, rationale, mitigation, and appeal or review path. Do not conceal a sacrifice behind a claim of universal benefit.
